📚 WA Education System Overview

The Western Australian education system provides quality learning opportunities for all children from early childhood through secondary school. The system is overseen by the Western Australian Department of Education.

🎓 Education Structure

Early Childhood Education

  • Kindergarten: Children can attend from the year they turn 4 by June 30
  • Pre-primary: Compulsory for children turning 5 by June 30
  • Focus: Social, emotional, and foundational learning development

Primary School (Ages 5-12)

  • Covers Pre-primary to Year 6
  • Emphasizes literacy, numeracy, science, and personal development

Secondary School (Ages 12-18)

  • Covers Years 7 to 12
  • Lower secondary: Broad curriculum
  • Senior years: Specialization for ATAR (Australian Tertiary Admission Rank), VET (Vocational Education and Training) pathways, or other qualifications

🏫 Types of Schools

Public (Government) Schools

  • Funded and managed by WA Department of Education
  • Free for Australian citizens and permanent residents
  • Students typically attend local school in their catchment area
  • Small voluntary contributions may apply

Private and Independent Schools

  • Charge tuition fees
  • May have specific religious or educational philosophies
  • Must meet curriculum and safety standards
  • Include Catholic and other independent schools

📅 School Year Structure

  • Four Terms: Generally runs from late January to mid-December
  • Curriculum: All schools follow the Western Australian Curriculum and Assessment Outline
  • Key Learning Areas: English, Mathematics, Science, Humanities and Social Sciences, The Arts, Health & Physical Education, Languages, and Technologies

📊 Assessment & Qualifications

  • NAPLAN: National Assessment Program for Literacy and Numeracy
  • WACE: Western Australian Certificate of Education (senior secondary) - required for university entry or vocational pathways
  • Regular classroom assessments and progress reports
📖 Official Resource: Department of Education, Western Australia

📝 School Enrolment Guide for Migrant Families

⚠️ Key Considerations for New Migrant Families:
  • Verify your visa/residency status to confirm eligible public schools
  • Determine your child's appropriate WA year level
  • Check school for available spaces and support (language/transition)
  • Some WA public schools accept international students (500 subclass visa) as fee-paying overseas students
  • If your visa qualifies for local enrolment (no full fees), confirm your visa subclass and rights

Step 1: Check Eligibility & Visa Status

  • Confirm your child's visa/residency: Australian citizen, permanent resident, or temporary visa holder with local enrolment rights
  • Student visa (500 subclass): Must follow international student enrolment pathway
  • Family or other visas: Confirm "local enrolment entitlement" with school or WA Department of Education

Resources:

  • Department of Education - Overseas Applicants
  • Student Visa Enrolment

Step 2: Identify Suitable Schools

  • Use the School Finder tool to locate schools accepting migrant/international children
  • For local-intake schools, check catchment zone for your residential address
  • For fee-paying/international pathways or out-of-catchment applications, select up to 4 preferred schools

Step 3: Gather Required Documentation

  • Child's identity: Passport, birth certificate (with English translation if needed)
  • Proof of address: Lease, utility bill (for local-intake schools)
  • Visa documents: For child and parent/guardian
  • School records: Overseas report cards for year placement assessment
  • Immunisation: Australian Immunisation Register records if required
  • Special needs: Documentation for English language support or special educational needs

Step 4: Submit the Application

  • Local public schools: Contact school directly about their overseas migrant enrolment process
  • International/fee-paying students: Apply via International Education portal, listing up to 4 preferred schools
  • Disclose family migration status, expected arrival date, or current location
  • Inquire about mid-year enrolment procedures if arriving partway through the year

Step 5: Determine Year/Grade Placement

  • Schools assess placement based on child's age and previous schooling
  • Prepare for transition support for different curriculum/language backgrounds

Step 6: Accept Offer & Finalise Enrolment

  • Confirm acceptance and submit final documentation (address, visa)
  • Attend welcome meetings/orientations for new migrant families
  • For Kindergarten/Pre-primary: Verify age and immunisation eligibility
  • Update school on any changes to address, visa status, or family situation

Step 7: Support & Adjustment

  • Ask about EAL (English as an Additional Language) support
  • Inquire about new arrivals programs for migrant children
  • Visit school, meet staff, and discuss academic/social support
  • Clarify transport options (buses, drop-off) for unfamiliar areas
  • Encourage participation in orientation to build peer connections

💡 Additional Tips for Migrant Families

  • Move early to start at beginning of a term (eases adjustment)
  • Keep original documents and certified English translations
  • For secondary students: Check subject options and WACE mapping
  • Confirm local public school for your target suburb to secure priority enrolment
  • Attend open days, check textbooks, uniforms, and extra fees (excursions, sports)
  • Ask about cultural support (migrant liaisons, multicultural staff)
  • Notify schools immediately if arriving close to February/March school year start

🌍 Supporting Multilingual Children

Raising a multilingual child involves understanding their development, creating supportive environments, and fostering positive language habits. Here's a structured breakdown:

1. Embrace Language Confusion as Normal

  • Language confusion (mixing words/grammar from multiple languages) is common and temporary
  • Children naturally learn to separate languages as they develop
  • Avoid pressuring them to "stick to one language"
  • Cultivate a positive attitude toward multilingualism to reduce anxiety

2. Build a Solid Home Language Foundation

  • Speak your native language consistently at home for stability
  • Use simple, clear sentences with repetition to reinforce understanding
  • Incorporate daily activities: read books, tell stories, and sing songs in your home language
  • Label household objects with native language words for visual learning

3. Support the New/Local Language

  • Encourage exposure via school, playdates, or community activities (e.g., English or local language)
  • Use dual-language books or apps to reinforce learning across languages
  • Correct gently: repeat the correct word/sentence instead of criticizing mistakes

4. Establish Language Routines

  • Allocate daily time for each language (e.g., home language at meals, local language for schoolwork)
  • Play interactive games: name objects, simple memory games, or language-matching activities

5. Promote Social Interaction

  • Let your child engage with peers who speak both languages
  • Join multicultural playgroups or community programs for diverse families
  • Social exposure naturally strengthens language skills and confidence

6. Model Positive Language Use

  • Show your child you value both languages (prioritize using them in daily life)
  • Demonstrate context-appropriate language switching (e.g., greeting relatives in native language, ordering food in local language)

7. Practice Patience & Observation

  • Avoid comparing your child to monolingual peers - multilingual development follows its own timeline
  • Track milestones in both languages; mixing is typically part of normal growth
  • Consult teachers or speech therapists if confusion persists or developmental delays are noticed

8. Utilize Support Services

  • Take advantage of school EAL (English as an Additional Language) programs
  • Access local library/multicultural center offerings: story time, language workshops, or resource hubs
  • Join parent workshops focused on supporting bilingual/multilingual children

🌟 Key Tip

Multilingual children often develop strong cognitive skills and cultural awareness. Celebrate their progress, keep language learning fun, and avoid unnecessary stress - initial confusion is a temporary part of building lifelong language abilities.

💚 Self-Care & Wellbeing

Taking care of yourself and managing your emotions is essential for a happy, healthy family. Here are practical strategies for maintaining your wellbeing.

🤝 Being an Accidental Counsellor

Core Principles

  • Help, don't rescue: Support others to explore options and make their own decisions
  • Show empathy: Listen without judgment and understand their emotions
  • Be present: Use paraphrasing, summarising, and reflective listening

Key Actions

  • Ask thoughtful questions to guide problem-solving
  • Know your limits - refer to professionals when needed
  • Practise self-care (yoga, walking, meditation) to stay grounded

😔 Understanding and Overcoming Loneliness

What It Is: Feeling disconnected despite being around people.

Impacts: Linked to poor health - comparable to smoking 15 cigarettes a day.

Causes

  • Busy lifestyles and longer work hours
  • Overuse of digital technology limiting real contact

Solutions

  • Disconnect from work to spend time with loved ones
  • Join clubs, volunteer, or pursue hobbies
  • Build casual, realistic friendships
  • Prefer calls or visits over social media
  • Seek help if loneliness becomes overwhelming (e.g. Lifeline 13 11 14)

🧘 Emotional Regulation

  • Notice thoughts and feelings: Be aware without judgment
  • Defuse unhelpful thoughts: Visualise them floating away or repeat them to reduce power
  • Stay present: Use breathing, senses, and movement to anchor yourself
  • Focus on what you can control: Act on small, positive steps

Tips

Stay active, use positive self-talk, organise your day, rest well, and be kind to yourself and others.

🛡️ Setting Healthy Boundaries

  • Know your limits: Assess physical, emotional, and mental needs
  • Communicate clearly: Use respectful, direct statements
  • Follow through: Reinforce boundaries and thank others for respecting them
  • Stay flexible: Adjust boundaries as needed

💼 Navigating Unemployment

Mindset: Job loss is temporary and not a personal failure.

For the Unemployed Partner

  • Communicate openly, manage finances together, and maintain routines
  • Stay proactive - network, apply, and stay positive

For the Employed Partner

  • Listen, reassure, and avoid pressure
  • Acknowledge your partner's feelings and household contributions

⚖️ Achieving Work-Life Balance

  • Simplify tasks and let go of perfectionism
  • Exercise, rest, and schedule screen-free time
  • Share home duties and set realistic priorities
  • Take leave, advocate flexible work, and model balance for children

🏘️ Building Community Connections (Neighbour Day)

  • Start small: smile, say hello, share food
  • Offer help: mail, bins, errands
  • Join local groups or host casual catch-ups
  • Support older or isolated neighbours
  • Organise or join community events like clean-ups or BBQs

😤 Managing Anger

  • Recognise anger: Ask what's behind it
  • Pause before reacting: Breathe and respond calmly
  • Own your feelings: Use "I" statements instead of blame
  • Reflect and repair: Learn from anger moments and apologise when needed

💑 Building and Sustaining Healthy Relationships

Healthy relationships bring joy but also challenges. Research - including John Gottman's decades of studies - shows couples thrive when they communicate respectfully, nurture trust, and manage conflict constructively.

Core Relationship Strategies

❌ Avoid Harmful Behaviours

  • Steer clear of Gottman's "Four Horsemen": criticism, defensiveness, contempt, and stonewalling
  • Communicate calmly, take responsibility, and show respect

🤝 Nurture Friendship

  • Treat your partner as your best friend
  • Express love, appreciation, and gratitude daily

🔒 Strengthen Trust and Commitment

  • Be reliable in good and bad times
  • Avoid comparisons - focus on your shared journey

😊 Maintain a Positive Perspective

  • Address issues at set times and focus on strengths
  • Acknowledge teamwork and progress

⚖️ Manage Conflict Effectively

  • Start gently, listen actively, and aim for win-win outcomes
  • Keep discussions respectful even when no solution is found

🔧 Repair and Forgive

  • Apologise sincerely and accept repair attempts

🎯 Create Shared Meaning

  • Build rituals, routines, and shared goals to deepen connection

💬 Enhancing Communication

  • Listen actively: Give full attention, make eye contact, and summarise what you hear
  • Show empathy: Validate your partner's feelings ("That sounds hard")
  • Use "I" statements: Express needs without blame
  • Manage emotions: Pause or take breaks when upset
  • Stay physically connected: A hug often says more than words

🤝 Handling Conflict

  • Approach disagreements as teammates, not opponents
  • Take "time-outs" when emotions rise (20+ minutes)
  • Keep communication respectful and stay on topic
  • Seek compromise and protect the friendship during disputes

💔 After a Relationship Breakdown

  • Healing takes time - seek support and avoid denial
  • Replace unhealthy coping (alcohol, isolation) with healthy outlets (exercise, friends)
  • Rebuild confidence through self-care and reflection
  • Avoid quick reconciliations or rushing into new relationships
  • Rediscover passions and personal purpose

💕 Starting New Relationships

  • Be curious, grateful, and responsive
  • Build trust through honesty and reliability
  • Communicate clearly and gently about issues
  • Face challenges together and celebrate small joys

✨ What Makes a Respectful Relationship

  • Communication: Share thoughts and feelings openly
  • Self-Care: Maintain mental, emotional, and physical balance
  • Conflict Resolution: Use "I feel" statements and listen without judgment
  • Positive Connections: Surround yourself with supportive people
  • Mindful Tech Use: Prioritise real-world connection over screen time
