Elders in the Classroom: Building Connection to Country

Location: Northern Territory, Australia

The Challenge: A remote primary school wanted to strengthen connections between Aboriginal students, their culture, and the curriculum while creating opportunities for all students to learn from Traditional Owners.

The Approach: The school established a weekly "Elders in the Classroom" program where local Aboriginal Elders teach language, storytelling, bush tucker, and traditional practices. Teachers co-plan lessons that connect these teachings to literacy, science, and arts learning areas.

The Impact:

  • Increased pride and cultural confidence among Indigenous students
  • Embedding of Indigenous perspectives across all subject areas
  • Strong partnerships with local Aboriginal organizations
  • Student leadership development and advocacy skills

Key Lesson: When students lead change, it creates deeper engagement and more sustainable outcomes. Adults must be willing to share power and listen to youth perspectives, even when uncomfortable.

Professional Development Systemic Change

Whole-Staff Cultural Competence Journey

Location: Regional Queensland

The Challenge: A school cluster recognized that sporadic cultural awareness sessions weren't creating lasting change in teacher practice or student outcomes.

The Approach: Five schools partnered to create a two-year professional learning program with Aboriginal education consultants. The program included: intensive workshops, classroom coaching, collaborative lesson planning, critical reflection circles, and community immersion experiences.

The Impact:

  • Measurable improvement in Aboriginal student literacy and numeracy results
  • Increased teacher confidence in embedding Indigenous perspectives
  • Development of culturally responsive classroom practices
  • Stronger relationships between teachers and Aboriginal families

Key Lesson: Cultural competence requires ongoing, deep professional learning—not one-off workshops. Supporting teachers to critically examine their own biases and assumptions is essential for real change.
