What does CALD mean?

CALD stands for Culturally and Linguistically Diverse

There are many kinds of CALD families:

  • From culturally diverse backgrounds
  • Speak English as an additional language or speak a language other than English
  • From refugee backgrounds
  • Born overseas
  • Have a parent born overseas

Understanding Key Concepts

🌍 Cultural Diversity

Cultural diversity means that people come from many different backgrounds and have their own ways of living.

  • We might speak different languages, eat different foods, celebrate different holidays, or believe in different things
  • Even if people are from the same culture, they can still have their own ideas and ways of doing things
  • That's what makes everyone special and interesting!

🗣️ Linguistic

Linguistic means that people speak many different languages. Some people speak one language, and others speak two or more.

Languages are an important part of who we are – they help us share our stories, connect with family, and understand our culture.

🏠 Refugee

A refugee is someone who has to leave their home country because it's not safe for them to stay there.

  • This might be because of war, danger, or unfair treatment
  • Refugees move to another country to find safety and start a new life
  • They often leave behind their homes, schools, and friends

🚫 RACISM

When people are treated unfairly because of the country they're from, their cultural background, what they look like, or the colour of their skin.


Can look like: Mean words, physical hurt, saying someone can't do something because of their skin colour, or nasty comments about their culture, language, food, or traditional costume.

🤝 INCLUSIVITY

Celebrating everyone in all their uniqueness. No matter how much you don't understand or are curious about their identity, you respect and uplift them.


Cultural Inclusivity: Recognising and respecting the diverse cultures within our environment.

💬 MICRO-AGGRESSION

Small comments or actions that can hurt someone – even if the person didn't mean to be unkind.


They might seem like "little things," but hearing them again and again can make people feel left out or disrespected. That's why it's important to think before we speak and treat everyone with respect.

🤔 PREJUDICE

Having a belief (usually negative) about someone or a group just because of who they are or where they come from, not because of what they've done.

📦 STEREOTYPE

Making an assumption that all people in a group are the same in some way – because they share a characteristic like language, culture, or appearance.


This is unfair because everyone is unique!

⚖️ DISCRIMINATION

Acting on beliefs or assumptions. When a person or group of people are treated badly or unfairly because of who they are.


Note: Racism is a type of discrimination.

💡 Why Does This Matter?

Understanding these concepts helps us create a world where everyone feels safe, respected, and valued. When we learn about different cultures and stand against racism, we make our schools and communities better for everyone!

Coping With Racism

How to Share Your Feelings and Get Support

Notice your feelings

  • What happened that upset you?
  • How does it make you feel?

Talk or write it down

  • Draw a picture, write a story, or make a list
  • A parent, teacher, or friend who listens and cares

Say what you need

  • Do you want advice, a hug, or just someone to listen?

Use "I" statements

  • "I feel sad when someone says that to me."

Coping With Racism

Self-Care

Stay connected to your culture!

Cook your favourite foods, listen to music, read books, or join cultural activities. It's a positive way to celebrate who you are and cope with racism!

Exercise!

Go for a run, stretching, yoga and group workout. Mindfulness: take 10-20 minutes to practice deep breathing exercise or meditation.

Jot it down!

Journal (morning, before bed or both). Write down positive affirmations/things that happened that day.

Taking a break from media!

Sometimes social media can get overwhelming, unplug if needed.

Finding strength in your beliefs!

Lean on your faith or belief system for comfort and perspective.

Learning & Reflecting!

Listen to podcasts about racism and diversity to understand more and feel connected.

Organisations & Support Network

Tips on How to Seek Help?

Think about what you would like to share. Speaking them out loud or writing thoughts on a piece of paper could help.

Some questions that could help:

  • What's on your mind?
  • What is your biggest concern?
  • How or why has it been bothering you?
  • How does it make you feel?
  • How is it affecting you?
  • What area of your life is this concern affecting the most?

Use 'I' statements when you are ready to speak to them!

So that they would know how you are feeling about the experience.

Be specific to what you would need from your trusted person!

  • Do you want someone to just listen?
  • Do you want advice, help, or support?
  • Being specific helps others know how to help you best.
